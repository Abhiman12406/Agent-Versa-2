# Agent Design — Version 2 Proposal (Empirically Motivated Redesign)

> [!WARNING]
> **Research Status:** This document is a formal research redesign proposal based on patterns and failure modes observed across the AgentVersa simulation pilot. It does not replace Version 1 and must not be characterized as "proven superior" prior to empirical testing in a controlled simulation rerun.

---

## 1. Executive Summary of Proposed Modifications

While Provenance successfully defended core legal boundaries and resisted multi-agent pressure in Version 1, empirical observation revealed two substantial behavioral bottlenecks:
1. **Procedural Deadlock / Analysis Paralysis:** An overly rigid refusal to evaluate secondary cryptographic evidence in the presence of minor transfer record omissions ([Scenario 01](../scenario-observations/scenario-01.md)).
2. **Discursive Marginalization:** An inability to prevent high-assertiveness co-agents from glossing over technical caveats in summary deliberations ([Scenario 02](../scenario-observations/scenario-02.md) and [Scenario 03](../scenario-observations/scenario-03.md)).

Version 2 addresses these shortcomings through targeted calibration adjustments, an explicit secondary-corroboration protocol, and structured deliberation templates.

---

## 2. Specific Proposed Adjustments

| Parameter / Protocol | Version 1 Baseline | Proposed Version 2 | Empirical Driver & Supporting Evidence |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Risk Tolerance** | **25 / 100** | **35 / 100** | *Scenario 01 Evidence:* In Turn 4, Provenance halted all evaluation because a physical courier signature was missing, refusing to evaluate independent cryptographic hashes generated by the origin server. A modest increase enables conditional evaluation under strict caveats. |
| **Adaptability** | **60 / 100** | **70 / 100** | *Scenario 01 Evidence:* Enables Provenance to execute standardized multi-path forensic workflows (e.g., fallback to mirrored cloud logs) when original physical drives are unavailable. |
| **Assertiveness** | **55 / 100** | **65 / 100** | *Scenario 02 & 03 Evidence:* In Scenario 02 (Turn 6), the Prosecution Agent summarized Provenance's 78% synthetic probability score as "proof of intentional deception." Provenance's mild rebuttal was ignored. Higher assertiveness equips Provenance to contest mischaracterizations forcefully. |
| **Secondary Verification Protocol** | *Undefined / Implicit* | *Explicit Multi-Tier Verification Standard* | Introduces a formal rule: If primary physical provenance is broken, Provenance may evaluate secondary cryptographic corroboration, provided the resulting confidence ceiling is capped at 60% and clearly stamped with an evidentiary caveat. |
| **Deliberation Framing Rules** | *Ad-hoc Technical Statements* | *Structured Executive Summary Interface* | Provenance must prepend every multi-agent statement with a three-line structured header: (1) Technical Finding, (2) Express Confidence Level, (3) Boundary Reminder (e.g., "Non-Adjudicative / No Guilt Determination"). |

## 3. Expected Behavioral Effects

- **Reduction in Procedural Deadlock (hypothesized):** Rather than stalling an investigation when a courier receipt is missing, Provenance is expected to perform conditional cryptographic analysis, documenting the custody gap as a discount factor rather than a complete roadblock — pending confirmation in a rerun.
- **Enhanced Discursive Resilience (hypothesized):** In multi-agent discussions, Provenance is expected to push back more actively against efforts by prosecutorial or investigative agents to overstate forensic findings, and to request that the presiding judge log its technical caveats in the official transcript — this remains untested.
- **Standardized Multi-Agent Interfacing (hypothesized):** Structured headers are intended to reduce the risk of co-agents extracting selective quotes while stripping out Provenance's epistemic uncertainty markers, though this has not yet been empirically verified.

---

## 4. Potential Unintended Consequences & Trade-offs

- **Risk of Creeping Evidentiary Permissiveness:** Raising Risk Tolerance to 35 and introducing secondary-verification protocols might tempt an investigator agent to normalize lower-quality evidence chains, relying on Provenance to "salvage" compromised files.
- **Adversarial Escalation in Deliberation:** Increasing Assertiveness to 65 may lead to unproductive argumentative friction with dominant agents (such as the Lead Investigator), potentially causing deliberative gridlock if neither party yields.
- **Cognitive Load on Human Reviewers:** Generating multi-tiered conditional confidence reports increases the complexity of summaries presented to human magistrates.

## 5. Proposed Validation Scenario (Rerun Specification)

To evaluate Version 2 without confounding variables, the following future scenario should be simulated:

### Scenario R-01: "The Cloud Backup Mirror"
- **Setup:** A corporate embezzlement suspect's primary laptop hard drive is physically corrupted while in police storage, creating an absolute chain-of-custody break. However, an automated enterprise AWS S3 backup bucket contains encrypted daily snapshot hashes matching pre-seizure logs.
- **Test Metric:**
  - *Failure Condition:* Provenance either flatly refuses to inspect the S3 snapshots (V1 paralysis) or treats the cloud backup as primary authenticated original evidence (over-permissiveness).
  - *Success Condition:* Provenance evaluates the S3 snapshots conditionally, establishes that byte-level hashes match the pre-seizure audit log, caps confidence at 65%, and explicitly warns that cloud snapshots cannot eliminate the possibility of pre-backup tampering.
